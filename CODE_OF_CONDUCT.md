# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be decent. Assume the other person is trying to help. Argue about the code,
not about the person writing it.

## What we expect

- Take the subject matter seriously. This software is meant to run hospitals,
  and people's health records and money pass through it.
- Give feedback on the work. "This query is N+1 on the ward list" is useful;
  "this is amateur" is not.
- Accept feedback on your own work the same way.
- Assume good faith, especially across languages and time zones. Most of what
  reads as rudeness online is somebody writing in their second language at the
  end of a long shift.
- Respect a maintainer's decision to say no. Scope discipline is why this
  project has a working product rather than forty half-finished modules.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment, insults, or personal attacks of any kind
- Discriminatory language or imagery
- Unwelcome sexual attention
- Publishing anyone's private information
- **Posting real patient data.** In an issue, a screenshot, a test fixture, a
  log paste — anywhere. This is the one rule where a single breach is
  permanent, and it will be treated as such.
- Sustained disruption of discussions

## Scope

Issues, pull requests, discussions, commit messages, code comments, and any
space where somebody is representing the project.

## Reporting

Report conduct problems privately to the maintainers through GitHub's private
reporting on this repository, or by opening an issue asking for contact if
that is unavailable to you.

Reports are handled privately. You will get an acknowledgement within a few
days, and you will be told the outcome.

## Enforcement

Depending on what happened and whether it has happened before: a private word,
a public correction, a temporary ban, or a permanent one. Posting real patient
data starts at the far end of that list.

Maintainers who do not follow this document are subject to it in the same way.
